# D-176 (2026-11-18)

## Decision

**Who may share a saved view, and what happens to shared views a person may no longer read**
(Haider, the product owner, through the coordinator; built in WP-046b S3a, #549).

1. **Two rights per record type, both editable per role:**
   - `<type>.share_views`: share with the support groups a person is in, and with members of
     those groups. Technicians, team leads (at `group` scope) and service desk managers by
     default.
   - `<type>.share_views_wide`: share with everyone and with other groups. Service desk managers
     (everywhere) and tenant administrators. Team leads hold it at `group` scope, meaning only the
     groups they lead (D-152), never everyone.
   - Requesters hold neither.
   - **Releases** (the coordinator's reading, 2026-11-19): the release manager plays the
     technician's part there, so `releases.share_views` goes to release managers and service desk
     managers, and `releases.share_views_wide` to service desk managers. Technicians and team
     leads keep only `releases.view` (WP-058); an administrator may grant more per role.
   - Views already shared stay as they are; only what a save adds to an audience is checked.
2. **Restricted or sealed filters:**
   - A shared view whose filter names a field some reader may not see (ADR-047 role rules, the
     problem root-cause gate) or a sealed one (ADR-050) is not listed for that reader, and opening
     it by ID is the same "not found" as a missing view (lane C's condition, the guarantee).
   - Sharing a view whose filter names a field any role restricts, or a sealed field, is refused
     on save (`view_filter_not_shareable`, naming the field and audience).
   - Existing offending shared views are made private to their owner by the view audit: nothing
     deleted, audited, the owner told which field caused it and how to share again, other people's
     stars removed. An administrators' report lists them, with a re-run.
   - The clean-up was first agreed as a data step in migration 0178; it was changed (the
     coordinator and lane C, 2026-11-19) to the audit job, which runs every six hours, on field
     rule and seal changes, and on request. Its first run after deploy completes the clean-up;
     nothing leaks meanwhile, because reading already hides offending views.
3. **Sealed filter values stored in views** are sealed in the follow-up after S3a (lane C's
   decision as encryption reviewer).

## Rationale

Shared views must never become a way round field access, and a share is a decision about who
sees a list, so it needs its own right instead of coming with the right to see the records.
Checking on every read keeps it right as roles and rules change; the job tidies up without
deleting anyone's work. Releases follow their change-control rules.
