# D-172 (2026-11-16)

## Decision

**CMDB: field access, affected users, history retention and MonetX** (Haider, the product
owner, through the coordinator; lane B's WP-053 plan).

- **(a) Fine-grained access is narrow-only.** Field and record rules only reduce what a role
  sees, and several rules combine as a union of what they withhold; a rule never grants
  anything (the ADR-047 pattern). The same rules cover assets too.
- **Editions.** Enterprise has only the custom rule builder (`fine_grained_access`, WP-053 S3).
  Professional's plain role scopes already include the person's own sites and own groups for the
  CMDB and for assets, with no Enterprise gate: `cmdb.view` (and `cmdb.edit`, `cmdb.delete`)
  take `all`, `site` (the CI's site is one of theirs), `group` (its owning or managing group is
  one of theirs) and `own` (they own it), in lists, the record (404 outside), the picker,
  `ci_refs` (hidden), search and realtime.
- **(b) Affected users are service subscribers:** the people in the departments, sites or
  groups linked to the affected business services, each person counted once.
- **(c) CI history is kept forever by default.** A tenant admin may set a retention of at least
  1 year; each clean-up is audited. Baselines are kept until someone deletes them. WP-053 S1
  writes a version row after every save and prunes none; retention comes in a later session.
- **(d) MonetX** is built against a fixture and a fake until there are API docs; the relay
  question waits.

## Rationale

Narrow-only rules cannot widen access by mistake, and a union is the safe way to combine them.
Counting subscribers once through the service's linked units matches how impact is reported.
Keeping CI history is what audits and "as of" views need, with a floor so a tenant cannot erase
its recent past. Without MonetX docs, a fixture keeps the connector testable without guessing.
