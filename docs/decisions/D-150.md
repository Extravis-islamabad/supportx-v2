# D-150 (2026-11-09)

## Decision

**Record lifecycle events go into the tamper-evident audit log (WP-064, lane B, about 4 sessions,
after its current asset work).** The product owner approved lane B's plan on 2026-11-09, with these
additions.

**Scope:**
- Incidents, service requests, problems, changes, releases and assets.
- One chained audit entry per save, written in the save's own transaction. It is named by its
  lifecycle events:
  - create, status change, assignment (group/technician), priority/impact/urgency change;
  - SLA pause/resume, resolution, closure, reopen;
  - approval decisions, link/merge, trash/restore/delete, attachment add/remove;
  - permission-relevant field edits.
- **When one save does several of these, the entry lists all of them.**
- A save matching none of them is recorded as `<type>.updated`.

**Values and verification:**
- Before/after values stay in record history (`field_changes`). The entry holds a fingerprint of
  the save's change set, so record history becomes tamper-evident.
- **The audit viewer** shows the before/after values from record history next to a "verified" mark
  computed from the stored fingerprint, and flags any mismatch clearly.

**Erasure:**
- Personal data (names, emails, free-text values) is kept outside the hash chain, referenced by ID,
  with only a salted hash in the chain.
- Erasure deletes or crypto-shreds the personal data. The chain stays verifiable and shows "erased
  on request" (en/ar/ur).
- The verifier accepts an erased reference only when a later `audit.subject_erased` entry exists.

**Legal hold:**
- Records under legal hold (and their audit entries and personal data) can't be erased or purged
  until the hold is lifted.
- Placing and lifting holds is itself audited.

**Retention per category** (defaults: record events 3 years, adjustable 1-10; security, access and
sessions 3 years, never below 1; reads 1 year):
- The admin guide and the installer prompt the admin to set retention to their regulator's
  requirement (banks often 5-10 years).
- Every retention change is audited.

**Older entries:** a one-off scan of existing audit entries for personal data stored inside the
chain **reports to the product owner before anything is masked**.

**Sealing:** throughput is raised to about 2,000 entries/s, proven by a 10M-entry benchmark.

**Not included:** no back-filled entries for existing records.

**Hook point:** one place, an audit sink port in `app/shared/history.record_changes`. Modules add
no calls; lanes A and C review the field lists for their record types.

## Rationale

The 2026-11-09 date repair showed that incident create, edit and status saves had no audit entry,
so the record history could not be verified against the signed chain. Fingerprinting the change
set gives named, tamper-evident lifecycle events without copying every value twice. Keeping
personal data outside the chain keeps erasure possible.
